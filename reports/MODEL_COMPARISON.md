# DengueShield AI: model comparison

Generated by `scripts/model_comparison/build_model_comparison.py` from saved results only (no metric is estimated or filled in).
Machine-readable table: `reports/MODEL_COMPARISON.csv` (one row per configuration × evaluation window × horizon, plus a 2–4 week mean).
All numbers are **validation** results on the organizers' synthetic data, 2016–2024. **2022–2024 were also used to choose the model, so they are not a blind test.** The 2025 test year is sealed and was never read.

## 2. How every model was evaluated

- **Season-level out-of-time folds.** For season *Y*, a model is fitted on all labels reported by 31 Dec *Y − 1* (expanding window from 2016) and predicts season *Y*; evaluation rows have both the forecast date and the target week in *Y*. Same rows for every model.
- **Convention B (production):** a forecast issued on the Monday of week *t* uses weather and cases up to week *t − 1*. Conventions A and C are kept in the CSV but never mixed with B.
- **Windows:** 2019–2024 (six seasons) and 2022–2024 (three seasons, also used for model selection). Tuned models are shown on 2022–2024 only because they were tuned on 2019–2021.
- **Features:** F8 (24: calendar, ward vulnerability, rain aligned to the target week) and F3 (38), frozen after Stage 2.
- **Metrics** (identical code for every row, `dengueshield/models/seasons.py::score`): MAE and RMSE of the point forecast (count models use the median of their predictive distribution; point models their forecast), R² of the expected-value forecast, bias, error at peak weeks (actual ≥ the training 90th percentile), outbreak recall, precision and false-alert rate (alert = point forecast ≥ that threshold), fit time and prediction time.
- **Two averages, both reported:** `mae`/`rmse` are the mean of the season values (the leaderboard definition, used for selection); `mae_pooled`/`rmse_pooled` pool every ward-week (the definition in `app_data/metrics_canonical.json`). They differ in the third decimal (champion, 2 weeks, 2022–2024: 1.107 season-mean vs 1.106 pooled). Tables below use the season mean unless labelled pooled.
- **Provenance check:** the per-cell scores reproduce the published leaderboard exactly (largest difference < 1e-9). R² is attributed by **exact artifact identity**: the tournament's cache key (a hash of model, feature columns, convention, parameters, horizon, season, rain projection and tag; tuned parameters read from the original Optuna databases) is rebuilt for each cell and the file's stored metadata must match the cell (1155 cells identified; MAE agreement is only a secondary check). Ensembles were never cached, so they have no R². The new benchmarks' R² comes from their own predictions.

## 3. The champion and why it was chosen

The rule was fixed **before** the tuned and ensemble results were seen (`docs/model_selection.md` §7): (1) eligible candidates have a predictive distribution and no failed fits; (2) shortlist everything within 0.05 MAE of the best on 2022–2024 that also holds on 2019–2024; (3) pick the simplest (plain Poisson GLM, then other GLMs, then ensembles, then boosting).

**Result: ridge Poisson GLM on F8 (24 features).** Average MAE 1.337 on 2022–2024 and 1.365 on 2019–2024 (R² 0.71 / 0.68). It is within the rule's 0.05 MAE selection tolerance of the best eligible model on both windows, has the lowest season-to-season variability of the GLM shortlist, the best peak-week error among the GLMs (4.42 on 2019–2024), a 24-feature design that explains every forecast additively, and a deployed bundle of 11 KB for all three horizons.

## 6. New classical benchmarks (V3)

Run by `scripts/model_comparison/run_classical_benchmarks.py` on the identical folds, rows, convention B, feature sets, outbreak thresholds and scoring code. Preprocessing is the GLM's (training-only median imputation, 0.5/99.5% winsorising, standardising). Ridge least squares and k-NN pick one setting inside every fit on its last training year; Random Forest, Extra Trees and the MLP use fixed settings chosen before any result. Nothing was tuned on a validation season.

**Reading.** None of the five beats the champion on either window; the closest on 2019–2024 is knn, F8, 0.045 MAE behind. Because they forecast a mean with a squared-error loss, the tree ensembles predict higher values (bias 0.02 to 0.13 against the champion's -0.07): they are slightly better at peak weeks (MAE 4.304–4.417 vs 4.425) and catch a few more outbreaks, but raise more false alerts (0.054–0.061 vs 0.045) and are worse on the many zero-case weeks (MAE 0.22–0.24 vs 0.17). Converting their point forecasts to Poisson medians (the integer forecast the count models use) does not close the gap, so the gap is not an artefact of scoring. As configured here, all five produce point forecasts only. Uncertainty intervals and an outbreak probability could be added to them (for example with quantile or conformal methods), but that was not built or validated in this benchmark, so as run they do not meet the deployment rule, which requires a predictive distribution.

Honest diagnostics:
- **knn**: chosen k at the grid edge (80) in 30 of 36 fits; grid kept fixed.
- **mlp**: 23.1% of predictions were negative and clipped to 0 (MAE before clipping reported); optimiser hit its iteration cap in 20 of 36 fits.
- **ridge_ls**: 29.2% of predictions were negative and clipped to 0 (MAE before clipping reported).
- **SARIMA was not run.** A fair per-ward seasonal ARIMA would need weekly seasonality (period 52) fitted for 60 wards in every season, on short, zero-heavy count series, with convention-B availability rules for any weather input. That cannot be done reliably or within reasonable compute here, so the time-series role is filled by the seasonal-average (climatology) and persistence baselines, run on the identical folds.

## 9. Limitations

- Only six out-of-time seasons. The rule treats candidates within 0.05 MAE as equivalent; 0.05 is a pre-set selection tolerance, not a confidence interval or significance test (paired season-level intervals are in `reports/model_tournament/paired_*.csv`).
- 2022–2024 informed model selection; the only untouched test is the sealed 2025 season.
- Point-forecast and distribution-median MAE are not identical quantities (columns `point_forecast` and `mae_poisson_median_of_point` make this visible).
- Model size is measured on one fit per family (season 2024, 3 weeks ahead, F8); fit times are mean seconds per season fit on this laptop and vary with hardware.
- Ensembles have no R² (predictions were combined on the fly and not cached).
- The data are synthetic; none of these numbers describe real-world surveillance accuracy.
